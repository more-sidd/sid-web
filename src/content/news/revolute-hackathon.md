Revolute was Boston's first robotics and physical AI hackathon, held
**1–2 August 2026 at FabLab Kendall** in Cambridge, MA, in partnership with
Boston Robot Hackers. The theme was cooking with robot arms.

Thirty participants worked in teams on roughly $15,000 of real robot hardware
supplied by Seeed Studio — not simulation, not toys. Each team had:

- a **reBOT Arm B601 RS**, a 48 V quasi-direct-drive arm with a 2.5 kg payload
- a **leader arm** for teleoperation and imitation learning
- a **reComputer Jetson Orin Nano (8 GB)** for on-edge inference
- overhead and wrist USB cameras

The two days ran from a hardware and software tutorial, through open hacking,
into a science-fair-style demo and judging session. Final submissions were a
GitHub repo, documentation and a one-minute video, judged by engineers from
Autodesk, Minoic Intelligence, Elemental Machines, Walden Robotics and
Vassar Robotics.

The point wasn't the competition. It was building Boston into somewhere the
robotics and physical AI community actually gathers — which only happens if
people keep showing up to organise it.
